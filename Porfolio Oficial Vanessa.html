The International Phonetic Alphabet

Evidence A – Definition

In my own words

The IPA is a universal alphabet where every symbol represents ONE specific sound. With IPA, you get /kæt/ vs /keɪk/. English spelling is a DISASTER, but IPA saves the day!

Case File // "The Sound Factory"

Phonemes & Speech Production

Evidence A – Definition

In my own words

A phoneme is the smallest unit of sound that changes meaning. /kæt/ vs /bæt/ — /k/ and /b/ are different phonemes. Pronunciation is how we say them.

Evidence B – My Examples
  • Minimal Pair Set/pɪn/ – /bɪn/ – /tɪn/ – /dɪn/
  • Sentence"The ship hit the sheep" → /ðə ʃɪp hɪt ðə ʃiːp/
Evidence C – Meme
Student: "I said SHIP, professor!!"
😱🚢😳
The /ɪ/ vs /iː/ phoneme: I am the most important thing in your life now
✦ phonemes matter in real life ✦

Case File // "Mission: Vocal Cords"

Speech Sound Production

Evidence A – Definition

In my own words

Lungs push air → larynx (vocal cords vibrate or not) → mouth shapes the sound. Voiced = buzz (/z/), voiceless = no buzz (/s/). Hand on throat = lie detector!

Evidence B – My Examples
  • Voiced/Voiceless pair/v/ vs /f/
  • Airstream demoSay /p/ with paper in front — it flies!

Case File // "How the Sound Escapes"

Manner of Articulation

Evidence A – Definition

In my own words

Plosives: block then BOOM (/p,b,t,d,k,g/). Fricatives: hissing (/f,v,s,z,ʃ,ʒ/). Nasals: air through nose (/m,n,ŋ/). Affricates: plosive + fricative (/tʃ,dʒ/).

  • Nasal testSay /m/ and pinch your nose — can't finish!

Case File // "Hiss, Pop, and Friction"

Fricatives & Affricates

Evidence A – Definition

In my own words

Fricatives: continuous friction (/f,v,θ,ð,s,z,ʃ,ʒ/). Affricates: stop + fricative (/tʃ,dʒ/). They come in voiced/voiceless pairs!

Evidence B – My Examples
  • All-fricative twister"Five fuzzy foxes felt the fresh forest breeze"
  • Affricate minimal pair/tʃɪp/ (cheap) vs /ʃɪp/ (sheep)
Evidence C – Meme
Fricative /ʃ/: smooth, effortless, just hissing
🐍😌 vs 💥🐍😤
Affricate /tʃ/: STOP. then hiss. dramatic. chaotic.
✦ affricates said: make it a whole event ✦

The Syllable

Evidence A – Definition

In my own words

A syllable is a beat of sound. Every syllable has a Nucleus (vowel), plus optional Onset and Coda. "Cat" = Onset /k/ + Nucleus /æ/ + Coda /t/.

Case File // "The Beat Goes On"

Strong & Weak Syllables

Evidence A – Definition

In my own words

Strong syllables are louder/longer/higher. Weak syllables use schwa /ə/. "baNAna" /bəˈnɑːnə/ — middle strong, ends weak.

Evidence B – My Examples
  • Stress changes meaning"REcord" (n) vs "reCORD" (v)
  • The photograph familyPHOtograph → phoTOgraphy → photoGRAPHic
Evidence C – Meme
English teacher: "every unstressed vowel becomes schwa /ə/"
🔍👀📖
Students: wait... they're ALL schwas?? what even is this language?
✦ schwa /ə/: the most powerful sound you never noticed ✦
